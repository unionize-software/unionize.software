# Page Templates

Use these as starting structures, not rigid scripts.

The templates define the questions a page must answer. They do not prescribe identical headings or sentence rhythms. Rename or combine sections when that produces a clearer page.

## Sourcing Notes

- Use structured `sources` metadata for any page that is `mixed` or `source-backed`, unless a body `Sources` section is clearly the better fit.
- Keep link text descriptive and specific.
- If a page needs a long "further reading" appendix, split the page instead of bloating it.

## Crisis Checklist Shape

Use for retaliation already in progress, layoffs, account safety, and other time-sensitive situations.

1. A two- or three-sentence orientation that names the immediate pressure.
2. `Do this first` in genuine priority order.
3. `Record the facts this way` with lawful-access and personal-device limits.
4. `Avoid these moves` with the reason each move creates risk.
5. `Move faster if...` with concrete escalation conditions.
6. `Get outside help` with jurisdiction-appropriate options and an explicit scope boundary.

Keep this page compact enough to use under stress. Prefer 350 to 650 words unless the legal distinctions require more space.

## Practical Playbook Shape

Use for pay, workload, surveillance, remote organizing, workplace mapping, and campaign-stage questions.

1. Open with a recognizable workplace situation.
2. Explain what may be happening and what would distinguish one explanation from another.
3. List the facts workers should compare or preserve.
4. Include one representative scenario or sample question.
5. Sequence the next moves instead of presenting an unranked list.
6. Explain common failure modes and the tradeoff behind each warning.
7. Close with a decision point and the next relevant guide.

Most playbooks should land between 700 and 1,200 words. Shorter is fine when the task is genuinely narrow.

## Rights Reference Shape

Use for protected activity, supervisor status, contractors, public-sector workers, and jurisdictional off-ramps.

1. State the plain-English orientation without promising a legal result.
2. Name the jurisdiction and the workers who may be outside it.
3. Give concrete covered, uncertain, and excluded examples.
4. Explain which facts change the analysis.
5. Link claims to primary or near-primary sources in context.
6. State what the page cannot decide and when the reader should seek qualified help.

Most reference pages should land between 700 and 1,300 words. Accuracy and boundaries matter more than completeness.

## Evidence Essay Shape

Use for scale, history, bargaining, and claims about the industry.

1. Name the claim being examined.
2. Show the evidence before giving the conclusion.
3. Explain at least one competing interpretation or limitation.
4. Separate historical description from strategic implication.
5. State what the evidence cannot establish about one workplace.
6. End with the practical lesson, not a slogan.

Most evidence essays should land between 1,000 and 1,800 words when the source base supports that depth.
