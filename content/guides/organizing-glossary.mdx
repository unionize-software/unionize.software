This glossary is here to make the rest of the site easier to read. The goal is not legal perfection in miniature. It is to give workers plain-language handles for the terms that come up most often.

## Protected concerted activity

Workers acting together about workplace conditions, or one worker genuinely seeking, preparing, or representing group action. The [NLRB's explanation](https://www.nlrb.gov/about-nlrb/rights-we-protect/the-law/employees/concerted-activity) also makes clear that coverage and conduct limits matter; the phrase is not a guarantee that every group-related act is protected.

## Majority structure

More than loud agreement. It means durable support across the actual workplace map, not just one cluster of friends or one active chat.

## Recognition

The point where a union becomes the bargaining representative through voluntary recognition or certification after a formal process. The [NLRB election overview](https://www.nlrb.gov/about-nlrb/what-we-do/conduct-elections) describes both routes. Recognition is not the same as having a contract.

## First contract

The bargaining phase after recognition or certification. This is where enforceable gains are fought over and where employer delay often becomes a major issue.

## Unit

The group of workers covered by a particular organizing or bargaining structure. Figuring out who is in or out can be contested.

## Misclassification

When a worker's label may not match the legal test that applies to the relationship. Tax, wage, labor, and state-law tests can differ.

## Supervisor exclusion

Under the NLRA, a worker with specified authority over other employees, exercised with independent judgment in the employer's interest, may be a statutory supervisor and excluded from employee coverage. The [statutory definition](https://www.nlrb.gov/guidance/key-reference-materials/national-labor-relations-act) is more specific than a title such as "lead" or "manager."

## Mapping

Understanding who works where, who talks to whom, what issues are shared, and where informal leadership actually sits.

## Escalation

Moving from private comparison toward broader action. Good escalation is structured. Bad escalation outruns the actual trust and support in the workplace.
